# Homepage Statistics

Verified September 18, 2026. These are external research estimates, not Gospel Advance outcomes.

## Current Measures

- 66%: [Lifeway 2017 survey, released 2019](https://lifewayresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Young-Adult-Church-Dropout-Report-2017.pdf). Surveyed U.S. adults ages 23-30 who regularly attended a Protestant church as teenagers; stopped regular attendance for at least one year between ages 18 and 22. Not necessarily permanent departure or loss of faith. No national headcount is supported.
- 3,810: [CDC Data Brief 549, January 2026](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db549.htm), Figure 2 data table. U.S. overdose deaths ages 15-24 in 2024, down from 5,926 in 2023. Do not substitute older larger totals or all-age figures.
- 40%: [CDC 2023 Youth Risk Behavior Survey](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-youth/mental-health/index.html). U.S. high school students reporting sadness or hopelessness almost daily for at least two consecutive weeks during the past year, enough to interrupt usual activities. Self-reported experiences, not clinical diagnoses or a college-student estimate. Replaces the medication-use statistic on September 21, 2026; receiving treatment is not itself a negative outcome.

Different populations and years: do not add these values together or present them as one Gen Z dataset. Keep age groups, years, sources, and caveats visible. No causal or treatment-replacement claims.

## Retired Measures (Not Displayed)

The following records document the earlier design, superseded by the measures above.

### College Mental Health

Source: [Healthy Minds Study 2024-2025, revised national report](https://healthymindsnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2024-2025-HMS-National-Data-Report_093025.pdf), printed pages 3-5 (PDF pages 6-8).

- 37%: moderate or severe depressive symptoms, PHQ-9 >= 10, previous two weeks.
- 33%: moderate or severe anxiety symptoms, GAD-7 >= 10, previous two weeks.
- 24%: often feeling isolated from others.
- Weighted estimates from 84,735 respondents across 135 participating colleges/universities. Not a census of Gen Z, diagnoses, or a measure of ministry impact.
- Use this revised report rather than the initial September release, which reported 32% for anxiety and 25% for isolation. Do not describe these data as evidence of rising rates without a comparable trend source.

### Separate Demographic Context

Source: [Williams Institute, August 2025](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/publications/trans-adults-united-states/), executive summary.

- 2.7%: rounded from 2.72%, estimated transgender share of U.S. adults ages 18-24, based on 2021-2023 BRFSS adult data and statistical modeling.
- Different population and measure from the college survey. Identity is not a mental-health or distress indicator; keep this separate from the mental-health counters.
